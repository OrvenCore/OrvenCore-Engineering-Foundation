# Identity and Access

> **Status:** Approved — 2026-08-15

Identity proves or asserts who or what is acting. Authorization decides what that actor may do. These are separate decisions:

```text
authenticated ≠ authorized
```

## Choose an identity model deliberately

Before implementation, a product **MUST** define:

- human, service, device, and automation identities;
- who may create, invite, disable, recover, and delete accounts;
- authentication methods and assurance appropriate to risk;
- authorization model and enforcement points;
- session/token lifetimes and revocation;
- privileged roles and step-up requirements; and
- audit events and support/recovery paths.

Privileged internal systems MAY use administrative provisioning instead of public registration. Public self-registration **MUST NOT** be enabled merely because a framework makes it easy.

## Account identifiers and lifecycle

- Internal subject identifiers **SHOULD** be stable, opaque, and independent of mutable email addresses or display names.
- Account creation, verification, role assignment, suspension, recovery, deletion, and data disposition **MUST** be defined.
- Disabled or deleted accounts **MUST** lose active sessions, tokens, and service credentials as soon as practical.
- Reassignment of email addresses or usernames **MUST NOT** transfer the previous account's permissions or data.
- Privileged account changes **MUST** be auditable.

Account enumeration through login, registration, or recovery responses **SHOULD** be minimized when it creates realistic privacy or attack risk.

## Passwords

Where passwords exist, implementation **MUST** follow current [NIST SP 800-63B-4](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html) and current OWASP password-storage guidance.

At minimum:

- passwords **MUST** be stored only with a maintained password-hashing library using a current salted, adaptive, memory-hard method and reviewed cost parameters;
- plaintext, reversible encryption, and fast general-purpose hashes **MUST NOT** store passwords;
- password input **MUST** support paste, password managers, and long passphrases, with a maximum accepted length of at least 64 characters;
- single-factor password authentication **SHOULD** require at least 15 characters; a shorter minimum MAY be used when a second factor is required, but **MUST NOT** be below current NIST guidance;
- common, expected, breached, and context-specific passwords **MUST** be rejected using a blocklist;
- arbitrary composition rules and periodic forced rotation **SHOULD NOT** be used;
- password changes **MUST** be required when compromise is known or reasonably suspected;
- password hints and knowledge-based security questions **MUST NOT** be used as authenticators; and
- comparison and error behavior **SHOULD** avoid useful timing or enumeration signals.

Hash cost **MUST** be benchmarked so verification is expensive for attackers but safe against service exhaustion. Parameters **SHOULD** be upgradeable during successful authentication.

## MFA and passkeys

- Privileged production, repository, CI, domain, secret-store, and identity-provider accounts **MUST** use phishing-resistant MFA where supported.
- Products handling sensitive data or high-impact actions **SHOULD** offer passkeys/WebAuthn or other phishing-resistant authentication.
- Recovery codes **MUST** be high entropy, single use, securely stored, and revocable.
- MFA enrollment, replacement, and removal **MUST** require strong verification and generate a security event.
- SMS or email MAY be a transitional or recovery factor when the threat model accepts its limitations; it **SHOULD NOT** be the strongest available factor for privileged access.

Recovery **MUST NOT** be materially weaker than normal authentication without additional controls and audit.

## Sessions

Session identifiers **MUST** be generated by a cryptographically secure source, have sufficient entropy, and contain no sensitive meaning.

- Sessions **MUST** be rotated after authentication, privilege elevation, recovery, and other fixation-sensitive transitions.
- Logout, password reset, account disablement, and security-driven revocation **MUST** invalidate relevant sessions.
- Idle and absolute lifetimes **MUST** be defined according to risk.
- Browser sessions **SHOULD** use server-managed, `Secure`, `HttpOnly` cookies with an intentional `SameSite` policy.
- Session state **MUST** be protected from tampering and replay appropriate to its design.
- Privileged and destructive actions **SHOULD** require recent or step-up authentication.
- Users **SHOULD** be able to inspect and revoke active sessions when the product risk justifies it.

Session tokens **MUST NOT** appear in URLs, logs, analytics, error reports, or referrer data.

## Tokens

Tokens **MUST** have a specific audience, purpose, issuer, subject or service identity, scope, lifetime, and validation policy.

- Signature or integrity, issuer, audience, expiry, not-before, and intended token type **MUST** be validated as applicable.
- The accepted algorithm or verification method **MUST** be configured; it **MUST NOT** be selected solely from untrusted token metadata.
- Access tokens **SHOULD** be short-lived.
- Refresh tokens **MUST** be confidential, revocable, scoped to the client, and rotated or sender-constrained when the architecture supports it.
- Tokens for one environment, audience, tenant, or purpose **MUST NOT** be accepted in another.
- Revocation, key rotation, and compromised-token response **MUST** be designed before release.

Opaque tokens MAY be preferable when immediate revocation and minimal data disclosure matter more than self-contained validation.

## OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect

OAuth/OIDC implementations **MUST** use a maintained, interoperable library and current security guidance, especially [RFC 9700](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9700/).

- Authorization Code flow with PKCE **MUST** be used for interactive clients unless a current standard defines a safer fit.
- The implicit grant and resource-owner password credentials grant **MUST NOT** be used.
- Redirect URIs **MUST** be pre-registered and matched exactly, except for narrowly specified behavior permitted by the current standard such as a native loopback client's ephemeral port. Unsafe wildcard matching **MUST NOT** be used.
- `state`, PKCE verifier/challenge, and OIDC `nonce` **MUST** be transaction-specific and validated where applicable.
- Native applications **MUST** use an external user agent rather than embedded web views for authorization.
- Clients **MUST** validate issuer, audience/client, signature, time claims, nonce, and authorization response binding as applicable.
- Authorization-server metadata **MUST** come from a trusted issuer and be validated.
- Scopes **MUST** be minimal and consent/administrative grants **MUST** accurately describe access.

An ID token proves an authentication event to its client; it **MUST NOT** be used as an API access token unless the API protocol explicitly defines that use.

## Authorization

Authorization **MUST** be:

- enforced inside the trusted service or process;
- deny-by-default;
- checked for every protected operation and object;
- tenant-aware where applicable;
- independent of UI visibility and client-side state; and
- tested for horizontal and vertical privilege escalation.

Roles MAY simplify administration, but permissions **SHOULD** express the action and resource actually controlled. Broad roles such as “admin” **SHOULD** be decomposed when their blast radius becomes difficult to understand.

Policy changes **MUST** invalidate or re-evaluate cached authorization within a documented bound.

## Service identities and API credentials

Each service or automation workload **SHOULD** have its own identity. Shared credentials obscure attribution and increase blast radius.

Service credentials **MUST** be:

- scoped to required operations and resources;
- attributable to one workload or owner;
- environment-specific;
- revocable and rotatable;
- auditable; and
- time-limited where the platform supports short-lived credentials.

Workload identity or short-lived federation **SHOULD** replace permanent API keys where practical. Unrestricted, non-expiring keys **MUST NOT** be the default.

## Administrative and production access

- Human production access **MUST** use individual accounts; shared administrator accounts **MUST NOT** be used for routine work.
- Privileged access **MUST** use MFA and least privilege.
- Access **SHOULD** be time-bound or elevated only when needed.
- Administrative interfaces **SHOULD** receive stronger network, device, authentication, and audit controls than ordinary user paths.
- Departing or reassigned personnel **MUST** have access reviewed and revoked promptly.
- Break-glass access MAY exist, but it **MUST** be tightly stored, monitored, tested, and reviewed after use.
